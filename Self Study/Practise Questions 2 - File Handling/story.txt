The lighthouse keeper woke before dawn every morning.
He climbed the spiral stairs one hundred and twelve steps.
At the top he checked the lamp and wiped the glass.
The sea below was calm and gray.
Gulls circled the rocks near the old pier.
A fishing boat passed slowly to the east.
He wrote the time and weather in his logbook.
Wind from the north, light rain expected.
After breakfast he repaired a broken shutter.
His radio crackled with news from the mainland.
A storm was forming far out in the Atlantic.
He spent the afternoon securing loose equipment.
Ropes were tied, doors were bolted, windows checked.
By evening the waves had grown much taller.
Rain hammered against the tower walls.
The lamp turned steadily through the long night.
Ships far away saw the beam and steered clear.
At sunrise the storm had finally passed.
The keeper made coffee and opened his logbook.
All ships safe, he wrote, and smiled.
